A comparison of [[09.1 - Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)|GANs]] and [[09.8 - Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)|VAEs]] against the **ideal properties of generative models**:

| Property | GANs | VAEs |
|---|---|---|
| Efficient sampling | ✅ | ✅ |
| High quality | ✅ | ❌ |
| Coverage | ❌ | ❓ |
| Well-behaved latent space | ✅ | ✅ |
| Interpretable latent space | ❓ | ❓ |
| Efficient likelihood | n/a | ❌ |

- **High-Quality Sampling**: GANs generate high-quality samples, often indistinguishable from real data.
- **Coverage**: GANs often struggle with coverage — they may represent only a subset of the training distribution (see **mode collapse** in [[09.1 - Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)|GANs]]). VAEs generally aim to cover the full distribution, though success here varies.
- **Well-Behaved Latent Space**: both models score well here — each latent variable $z$ corresponds to a realistic sample $x$, and smooth changes in $z$ produce smooth changes in $x$.
- **Interpretable Latent Space**: neither model excels here. Ideally, each latent dimension would control one understandable feature of the data, but neither GANs nor VAEs achieve this cleanly.
- **Efficient Likelihood Computation**: VAEs, despite being probabilistic, struggle to compute likelihoods efficiently. GANs don't use likelihoods at all, so this property doesn't apply to them.
