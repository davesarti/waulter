**Autoencoders** are unsupervised models that learn to represent input data in a reduced-size latent space and then reconstruct it, via two components: the **Encoder** and the **Decoder**.

The **Encoder** is a neural network that compresses the input into a lower-dimensional latent representation $z$, keeping only the most relevant features. The **Decoder** decodes $z$ back to the input's original size, producing a reconstructed sample $x'$. The reconstruction is compared to the original input $x$ via a **reconstruction loss** (L2 norm).

Training optimizes encoder and decoder weights jointly to minimize this loss: 
- the encoder learns to extract essential features; 
- the decoder learns to reconstruct from compressed features. 
-After training, the decoder is typically **discarded**, and the encoder can be reused to initialize a supervised model that predicts labels from the $z$ feature space.

## Bottleneck and Regularization

Keeping the bottleneck $z$ small forces the autoencoder to learn an intelligent, compact representation. An alternative way to force better representations is **noise injection**: add random noise to the inputs and train the model to recover the original noise-free data — this is the **Denoising Autoencoder**

## Convolutional Autoencoders

In many modern architectures, the encoder is a **CNN** that compresses data into a compact representation. The decoder mirrors the encoder's structure but uses **Transposed Convolution Layers**, which work in the opposite direction of standard convolutions: instead of reducing spatial size, they increase it — reconstructing the image from the compressed representation.
