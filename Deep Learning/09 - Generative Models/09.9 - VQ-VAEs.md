In a classic [[09.8 - Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)|VAE]], the prior $p_\theta(z)$ and posterior $q_\phi(z|x)$ are assumed to be normally distributed with diagonal variance — a **continuous** latent space. **VQ-VAE**  instead uses **discrete latent variables**.

## Discrete Latent Space

The posterior and prior distributions are **categorical**, and samples drawn from them index an **embedding table**:

1. The Encoder models a categorical distribution.
2. Sampling from it yields integer indices.
3. These indices look up vectors in an **embedding dictionary**.
4. The indexed embedding vectors are passed to the Decoder.

## Why Discrete?

For many kinds of data, discrete categories are a more natural fit than a continuous space. For example, image content may naturally fall into categories like "Dog" or "Bicycle" — mixing such categories in a continuous latent representation doesn't make semantic sense. Discrete representations are also simpler to handle: each category is a single, unambiguous value, whereas a continuous latent space requires normalizing density functions and reasoning about relationships between variables — considerably more complex.

## Dictionary-Based Representation

The **codebook** (embedding dictionary) can be thought of as a dictionary: each input is quantized to its closest vector (centroid) in this dictionary. This means each latent code is discrete, and can be visualized as being assigned to a particular cluster center. Optionally, sparsity can be imposed — or the number of active codes limited — to encourage a more compressed, structured representation. This results in a latent space that is more expressive and structured compared to traditional VAEs.

## Combining VQ-VAE with Autoregressive Models

To unlock the full potential of VQ-VAE, researchers combined it with **autoregressive models**, achieving high-quality, realistic image generation:

- Once a VQ-VAE is trained, it encodes images into discrete latent codes (selected from the codebook).
- These discrete codes can then be modeled autoregressively — i.e., we learn the distribution of latent codes across the training data.
- This turns image generation into a **sequence modeling task** over discrete tokens.

**Autoregressive generation** happens token-by-token, similar to how language models generate text: given a partially generated sequence of latent tokens, the model predicts the next token based on the previous ones. This is typically done using:
- Masked CNNs (e.g., PixelCNN)
- Recurrent neural networks (less common today)
- Transformers (e.g., in DALL·E and VQGAN setups)
