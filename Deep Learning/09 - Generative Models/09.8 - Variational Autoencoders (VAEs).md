Traditional [[09.7 - Autoencoders|autoencoders]] only replicate input data from the same dataset. **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)** enhance this by learning *distributions* over latent features, enabling generation of unique samples similar to — but distinct from — the training data. VAEs create a **continuous latent space** that supports sampling and interpolation.

VAEs are **explicit density estimation** models (unlike [[09.1 - Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)|GANs]]): they directly estimate the data likelihood:

$$p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(z)\,p_\theta(x|z)\,dz$$

where $z$ is the latent representation. Training maximizes this likelihood over $\theta$, so generated data fits the training data.

## The Intractability Problem

This integral cannot be computed directly, because we have no direct access to the data being integrated over $z$. The formula involves two distributions:
- $p_\theta(z)$ (**prior**): the latent space distribution — a simple Gaussian Multivariate, since the Decoder must be able to sample from it easily.
- $p_\theta(x|z)$ (**Decoder**): samples $z$ from the prior and transforms it into $x$, similar to the training data.

We cannot directly produce $p_\theta(z)$, since it depends on training data. Bayes' theorem gives a route to it via the posterior:

$$p_\theta(z|x) = \frac{p_\theta(x|z)\,p_\theta(z)}{p_\theta(x)}$$

but the denominator $p_\theta(x)$ is itself intractable — a circular problem.

**Solution — throw in a neural network.** The **Encoder** is trained to map an input to the latent space, $q_\phi(z|x)$, approximating the true posterior $p_\theta(z|x)$. This is only an *approximation*: it provides a **lower bound** on the data likelihood, not an exact solution.

## Architecture

The Encoder learns the mean and variance of the Gaussian latent distribution $z$. The Decoder learns the mean and variance of the generating distribution, matched to the dataset. A VAE can thus be seen as an autoencoder with a **probabilistic component** in place of a deterministic bottleneck.

## Deriving the Objective (ELBO)

Starting from log-likelihood and applying Bayes' rule and a few transformations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log p_\theta(x) = \mathbb{E}_z[\log p_\theta(x|z)] - D_{KL}\big[q_\phi(z|x)\,\|\,p_\theta(z)\big] + D_{KL}\big[q_\phi(z|x)\,\|\,p_\theta(z|x)\big]
\end{aligned}
$$

The third term (a KL divergence involving the intractable true posterior $p_\theta(z|x)$) cannot be computed and is dropped — but since KL divergence is always $\ge 0$, dropping it turns the remaining two terms into a valid **lower bound** on the likelihood (the ELBO — Evidence Lower BOund).

| Term | Name | Role |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{E}_z[\log p_\theta(x\|z)]$ | **Reconstruction Term** | Trains the Decoder to produce meaningful samples from $z$ |
| $D_{KL}[q_\phi(z\|x)\,\|\,p_\theta(z)]$ | **Regularization Term** | Computable in closed form (Gaussian vs. Gaussian); forces the latent space to match the Gaussian prior |

Together, these two terms make the latent space **continuous and well-structured**, enabling smooth interpolation between latent points without gaps.

**Three training regimes and their effect on the latent space:**

| Objective used | Resulting latent space |
|---|---|
| Reconstruction term only (plain autoencoder) | No structure — points scattered and disorganized; decoding a random/noisy point gives meaningless output |
| Regularization term only | Gaussian-shaped overall, but different classes' points overlap and mix, hard to distinguish |
| Both terms jointly (VAE) | Continuous, Gaussian-shaped, **and** well-separated clusters per class — the desired outcome |

## The Reparameterization Trick

Training with backpropagation requires differentiating through the sampling step, but sampling $z \sim q_\phi(z|x)$ directly is **not differentiable**. The fix: sample $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ from a fixed standard normal, and compute

$$z = \mu(x) + \sigma(x) \cdot \epsilon$$

This makes the sampling process differentiable with respect to the model's parameters ($\mu$, $\sigma$), enabling end-to-end training of the latent representation.

## Conditional VAEs (CVAEs)

Sometimes we want to generate data conditioned on additional information — class labels (e.g. digit identity for MNIST), attributes (e.g. "smiling" or "blond hair"), or even text descriptions — rather than leaving the model free to sample any point of the learned distribution. Just like [[Conditional GAN (cGAN)|Conditional GANs]], VAEs can be extended to handle conditioning.

To condition a VAE, the conditioning variable $y$ is added to both the Encoder and the Decoder: the decoder distribution $p_\theta(x|z)$ becomes $p_\theta(x|z,y)$, and the encoder approximation $q_\phi(z|x)$ becomes $q_\phi(z|x,y)$. The derivation of the ELBO stays exactly the same — $y$ is simply carried through both terms. This lets the VAE generate outputs guided by specific attributes or descriptions, giving much finer control over generation than the unconditional model.
