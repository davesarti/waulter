**Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)** are probabilistic generative models that rely on **implicit density estimation**: instead of explicitly modeling $p_{model}(x)$, GANs learn to *sample* from it using an adversarial approach between two neural networks — the **Generator** and the **Discriminator**.

The **Generator** learns to transform random noise into complex samples that resemble a target distribution (e.g., real images). This process is supervised by the **Discriminator**, which simultaneously learns to distinguish real data samples from those produced by the Generator ("fake" samples).

The adversarial aspect is crucial: the Generator tries to fool the Discriminator, while the Discriminator tries to correctly tell real from fake. This drives both networks to improve iteratively, as any improvement in one requires a corresponding adjustment in the other.

The Generator takes random noise $z$ from a simple distribution $p_z$ and transforms it into fake samples $x_{fake}$. The Discriminator receives both real samples $x_{real} \sim p_{data}(x)$ and fake samples, and classifies them as real or fake via $D(x)$.

## Objective Function of GANs

The Generator and Discriminator are jointly trained through a **min-max optimization problem**:

$$\min_{\theta_g} \max_{\theta_d} \; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{data}} \big[\log(D_{\theta_d}(x))\big] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}\big[\log(1 - D_{\theta_d}(G_{\theta_g}(z)))\big]$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\theta_d, \theta_g$ | Discriminator / Generator parameters |
| $D_{\theta_d}(x)$ | Discriminator output for real data |
| $D_{\theta_d}(G_{\theta_g}(z))$ | Discriminator output for generated (fake) data |

- **Discriminator** ($\theta_d$): maximizes the objective, wants $D(x) \to 1$ (real) and $D(G(z)) \to 0$ (fake).
- **Generator** ($\theta_g$): minimizes the objective, wants $D(G(z)) \to 1$ (tricking the Discriminator into believing $G(z)$ is real).

## Training GANs

Training alternates **Stochastic Gradient Descent** between gradient ascent on the Discriminator and gradient descent on the Generator.

Still, GAN objective is hard to optimize because its loss doesn't track sample quality, and early in training the Generator's gradient is weakest exactly where it needs to learn most (when samples are still obviously fake) and strongest where learning is no longer useful (when samples already fool the Discriminator) — so the **non-saturating trick** fixes this by having the Generator maximize instead of minimizing , giving it a much stronger learning signal early on.

$$\max_{\theta_d} \; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{data}} \big[\log(D_{\theta_d}(x))\big] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}\big[\log(1 - D_{\theta_d}(G_{\theta_g}(z)))\big]$$
$$\max_{\theta_g} \; \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}\big[\log(D_{\theta_d}(G_{\theta_g}(z)))\big]$$

## Inference using GANs

After training, the Discriminator is discarded and the Generator alone produces samples from random noise. Conceptually, the Discriminator is just an auxiliary object used to match the Generator's learned distribution to the data distribution.

## Open Problems

- **Overfitting and Control**: no effective tools exist to detect overfitting or control it during training.
- **Evaluation**: no standardized, reliable metric for GAN quality 
- **Training stability**: GAN training is notoriously unstable
- **Mode collapse**: the Generator, instead of capturing all modes of the target distribution, may collapse onto a single sub-mode. A healthy Generator gradually converges its distribution onto *all* modes of the target, while a collapsed Generator repeatedly commits to a *single* mode at each step, never spreading probability mass across the full target distribution.
