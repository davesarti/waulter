The loss function plays a key role in [[09.1 - Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)|GANs]]: different losses correspond to different ways of comparing the real and generated distributions. Two notable alternatives to the standard GAN loss are **LSGAN** and **Wasserstein GAN**.

## LSGAN

Cross-entropy is replaced with a **Least Squares Loss**:

$$\min_{\theta_d} \; \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{data}}\big[(D_{\theta_d}(x) - 1)^2\big] + \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}\big[D_{\theta_d}(G_{\theta_g}(z))^2\big]$$
$$\min_{\theta_g} \; \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}\big[(D_{\theta_d}(G_{\theta_g}(z)) - 1)^2\big]$$

This produces higher-quality generated images and Generators that are more robust to **mode collapse**, compared to regular GANs at the same training step.

## Wasserstein GAN (WGAN)

Minimizing the standard [[09.1 - Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)|GAN]] objective with an *optimal* discriminator is equivalent to minimizing the **Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence** between the real and generated distributions:

$$D_{JS}(P, Q) = \frac{1}{2}D_{KL}(P \| M) + \frac{1}{2}D_{KL}(Q \| M), \qquad M = \frac{1}{2}(P+Q)$$

where $P = p_{data}$ — the real data distribution, $Q = p_g$ — the generator's distribution.

Once the discriminator reaches its optimum, the generator is effectively forced to minimize this JS distance. The problem: when the real and generated distributions have little or no overlap (common early in training), JS divergence saturates and its gradients **vanish** — the generator gets no useful learning signal.

WGAN changes the loss using the **Wasserstein distance** instead of JS divergence — a metric from optimal transport theory that measures the minimum cost of transporting mass to convert one distribution into another.

Aligning the real and generated distributions with the Wasserstein distance provides **better (linear, non-vanishing) gradients and more stable training** than the original GAN formulation. Combined with the [[09.2 - DCGANs|DCGAN]] architecture, WGAN achieves a result no prior GAN could: sample quality that correlates with the loss value.
