Evaluating a [[09.1 - Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)|GAN]] is hard: there is no clear method to assess the visual quality of generated images. The first attempt was **human feedback**, which is subjective and costly.
Moreover, beyond individual sample quality, we also care about the **overall distribution** of generated samples matching the dataset.

Evaluation may split into two properties:

- **Recognizability**: does the GAN generate recognizable objects? If images of the same class produce a concentrated (low-entropy) label distribution, each image is confidently associated with that class.
- **Variety**: does the GAN, globally, produce a diverse range of objects?

Both properties are measured via a classifier's output probabilities (e.g., Inception-v3).

## Inception Score

The **Inception Score** is computed from the output of an image classifier, and is maximized when:
- the entropy of the label distribution *per generated image* is **minimized** (recognizable), and
- the classifier's predictions are **uniformly distributed** across all labels *over the whole generated set* (variety).

> [!warning] Overfitting problem
> A GAN that simply memorizes and reproduces training data can still score well on the Inception Score — such a model would not be useful, since it just copies images it has already seen.

$$
IS(G) = \exp\left(\mathbb{E}_{x \sim G}\left[D_{KL}\big(p(y|x) \,\|\, p(y)\big)\right]\right)
$$

where:
- $x \sim G$ — images sampled from the generative model
- $p(y|x)$ — conditional label distribution given image $x$, as predicted by the Inception model
- $p(y)$ — marginal class distribution over all generated images

## Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)

**FID** addresses this by comparing real and generated samples in a learned feature space:

1. Pass both real and generated samples through a classification network.
2. Take the activations of a chosen layer and treat them as multivariate normal distributions.
3. Compute mean and covariance for both sets, and compare them via the **Multivariate Normal Fréchet Distance**.

$$
FID(x, g) = \|\mu_x - \mu_g\|_2^2 + \text{Tr}\left(\Sigma_x + \Sigma_g - 2(\Sigma_x \Sigma_g)^{1/2}\right)
$$

FID reduces (but does not fully eliminate) the overfitting problem of the Inception Score.

**Advantages over IS:**
- Accounts for both quality and diversity
- Compares generated images to real ones (not just predictions)
- More sensitive to mode collapse

## Other metrics (overview)

Beyond IS and FID, evaluation can be grouped into three perspectives:

**Classifier's view**
- **Kernel Inception Distance (KID)**: like FID, but uses Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) with a polynomial kernel instead of only mean/covariance — captures higher-order statistics and is more robust with small sample sizes.

$$
KID = MMD^2 = \frac{1}{m(m-1)}\sum_{i \neq j} k(x_i, x_j) + \frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i \neq j} k(y_i, y_j) - \frac{2}{mn}\sum_{i,j} k(x_i, y_j)
$$

  with polynomial kernel $k(x,y) = \left(\frac{1}{d}x^\top y + 1\right)^3$, where $x$ are features from real images and $y$ are features from generated images.

- **Precision & Recall for GANs**: 
	- Precision = fraction of generated samples close to the real data manifold (quality); 
	- Recall = fraction of real samples covered by generated ones (diversity). 
	Both in $[0,1]$, higher is better. (Manifold-based, no closed-form equation.)

**Semantic view**
- **Perceptual Path Length (PPL)**: measures smoothness of the latent-to-image mapping by interpolating between latent points and measuring perceptual distance between outputs; lower PPL = smoother, more semantic latent space.

$$
l_{\mathcal{Z}} = \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon^2} d\big(G(\text{slerp}(\mathbf{z}_1, \mathbf{z}_2; t)),\, G(\text{slerp}(\mathbf{z}_1, \mathbf{z}_2; t+\epsilon))\big)\right]
$$

  where **slerp** is spherical interpolation between latent vectors, $t \sim U(0, 1-\epsilon)$ is the interpolation coefficient, $\epsilon$ is a small step size (e.g., 0.01), and $d$ is a perceptual distance metric.

- **Density & Coverage (D&C)**: kNN-based refinement of Precision/Recall
	- Density = how tightly generated samples cluster near real ones (fidelity);
	- Coverage = how much of the real distribution's modes are reached (diversity).

**Perceptual view**
- **MS-SSIM**: structural similarity across multiple scales, used to assess diversity/structural variation among generated samples.

$$
MS\text{-}SSIM(x,y) = [l_M(x,y)]^{\alpha_M} \cdot \prod_{j=1}^{M} [c_j(x,y)]^{\beta_j}[s_j(x,y)]^{\gamma_j}
$$

  where $M$ is the number of scales, $l$ is luminance, $c$ is contrast, and $s$ is structure.

- **PSNR / SSIM**: more common for super-resolution than generative evaluation.

**Other**
- **Downstream task performance**: e.g., train a classifier on generated data and check how well it performs on real data.
