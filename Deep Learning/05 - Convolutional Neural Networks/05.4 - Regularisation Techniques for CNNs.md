## Context

CNNs demonstrate excellent learning capability on computer vision tasks, but like other ML models they are **susceptible to overfitting**. Beyond the [[04 - Regularization|general regularisation techniques (L1/L2, Dropout, etc.)|]], several CNN-specific methods have been developed that operate directly on the input image or feature maps.

---

## Cutout

Randomly **cuts a rectangular region (16×16) from the input image** during training. The cut region is filled with the **average pixel value of the entire dataset**.

> *In practice: the model is forced to consider more parts of the image during training, thus reducing the risk of over-relying on a single discriminative patch.*

---

## Dropblock

An extension of [[04.9 - Dropout|Dropout]] adapted for convolutional layers. Instead of deactivating individual neurons, **entire contiguous regions of neurons within a convolutional feature map are deactivated**.

This prevents the network from focusing too much on specific spatial input features, promoting a more robust and general feature representation.

---

## Cutmix

During training, **a random rectangular area of an image is replaced with the corresponding area from another training image**. Class labels are then **mixed proportionally** to the replaced area.

This technique:
- Encourages the model to learn useful features from **multiple regions** of the image
- Provides a form of data augmentation + regularisation simultaneously
- Prevents the model from ignoring background regions
