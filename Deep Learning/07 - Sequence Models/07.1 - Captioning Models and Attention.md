Image captioning is the process of generating a textual description for given images. It can be seen as a **[[06.1 - Handling Variable-Length Sequences|Sequence to Sequence]] problem** from beginning to end: images (considered as sequences of pixels) are transformed into sequences of words. This task requires processing both textual and image statements:

- **Textual part** → Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)
- **Visual part** → Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), to obtain feature vectors

## BLEU Score

The **BLEU score** is a metric used to evaluate the quality of texts generated by NLP models.

- The text generated by the model is called **Candidate**
- The possible correct texts are called **References**

BLEU compares the candidate with references to measure similarity. It ranges from **0 to 1** and is based on the **proportion of matching n-grams** (sequences of n words, usually n = 1–4) found in the candidate, compared to the total number of n-grams in the candidate.

**Key adjustments:**
- **Occurrence limit**: the count of each n-gram is capped at the number of times it appears in the reference, preventing the model from scoring well by repeating the same n-gram
- **Brevity penalty**: the final score is multiplied by a penalty factor to prevent short translations from getting inflated scores

> **Example:**
> Reference: *"Transformers: Transformers make everything quick and efficient."*
> Candidate: *"Transformers Transformers Transformers Transformers."*
> → BLEU is low: the candidate repeats one word without providing full context

## Types of Attention

There are two main types of attention mechanisms: **soft** and **hard**.

> [!info] Soft Attention
> The context vector $z_t$ is computed as a **weighted combination** of image features, where weights $\alpha_{i,t}$ are the attention coefficients:
> $$z_t = \sum_i \alpha_{i,t} a_i$$
> During generation, different words become associated with different regions of the image. The network **dynamically focuses on specific parts of the image** as it generates each word, using a weighted combination that emphasises the most relevant regions.

> [!warning] Hard Attention
> The attention map gives score **1** to the single highest-weight location and **0** to all others — selecting a specific location in the image. The context vector $z_t$ is obtained by picking the corresponding cell in the feature map.
> Training is considerably more complex than soft attention, and the performance gain is often not significant. As a result, **hard attention is often neglected** in practice, while soft attention can be trained with standard SGD.
