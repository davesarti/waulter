## Core Idea

Dropout **randomly turns off some neurons** during the forward pass of the network. This process is **stochastic** — the choice of neurons to deactivate is random at each step.

By forcing the network to work with redundant representations, Dropout:
- Prevents hidden neurons from fitting too closely to each other
- **Forces neurons to focus on extracting more useful features**

---

## Mechanism

At each training step:
1. A **binary Dropout mask** is randomly extracted for each neuron
   - `1` → neuron is **active**
   - `0` → neuron is **deactivated**
2. The mask is applied during the **forward pass**
3. Each training example uses a **different Dropout mask**

---

## Training vs. Testing

| Phase | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Training** | Random mask applied at each step |
| **Testing** | Ideally integrate all masks (computationally expensive) |
| **Testing (practical)** | **Monte Carlo approximation** — many forward passes with different masks, average predictions |

This procedure is equivalent to **taking the average of all possible neural networks** sharing the same parameters.

---

## Relation to Bagging

Dropout is similar to [[04.8 - Model Ensembles|Bagging]] but more practical:
- Bagging: defines $k$ different models and $k$ different datasets (sampled with replacement)
- Dropout: **approximates this process with exponentially large numbers of neural networks** that share parameters — no need to train each one separately
