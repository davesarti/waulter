## Core Idea

Parameter norm penalties add a **penalty term to the loss function** during training, affecting the size (magnitude) of the model's weights. The general regularized loss function is:

$$\tilde{L}(\mathbf{W}) = L(\mathbf{W}) + \lambda\Omega(\mathbf{W})$$

Where $\lambda$ is the **hyperparameter controlling the importance of regularization** — it determines how much weight to give to the penalty relative to training performance.

## L2 Regularization (Ridge)

Adds a term proportional to the **square norm of model weights**:

$$\Omega(\mathbf{W}) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_l ||\mathbf{w}_l||^2$$

Full regularized loss:

$$\tilde{L}(\mathbf{W}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} L(f(x_i, \mathbf{W}), y_i) + \frac{\lambda}{2} \sum_l ||\mathbf{w}_l||^2$$

**Effect**: Weights tend to **smaller values** — none become exactly zero. Promotes simpler models by **uniformly reducing weight magnitudes**.

---

## L1 Regularization (Lasso)

Adds a term proportional to the **absolute norm of model weights**:

$$\Omega(\mathbf{W}) = \sum_l |\mathbf{w}_l|$$

Full regularized loss:

$$\tilde{L}(\mathbf{W}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} L(f(x_i, \mathbf{W}), y_i) + \lambda \sum_l |\mathbf{w}_l|$$

**Effect**: Favors **sparsity** — many weights tend to become **exactly zero**. Useful for **feature selection** as it makes the most relevant features more obvious.

---

## L1 vs L2 Comparison

| Property | L1 (Lasso) | L2 (Ridge) |
|---|---|---|
| Penalty shape | Diamond (absolute value) | Circle (squared) |
| Weight behavior | Sparse (many → 0) | Small but non-zero |
| Feature selection | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| Solution uniqueness | Not always | Always |

The **overlap between the error gradient and the norm penalty gradient** is the optimization point where the right balance between error reduction and model complexity is achieved.
