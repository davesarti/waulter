## The Hierarchy

AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL — three nested disciplines, each a specialization of the one above.

## Artificial Intelligence (AI)

**AI** is the broadest and oldest concept. It refers to the discipline aimed at enabling computers to mimic aspects of human intelligence.

Its roots trace back to the 1958 paper *"Programs with Common Sense"* by **John McCarthy**:

> *"Our ultimate objective is to make programs that learn from their experience as effectively as humans do."*

## Machine Learning (ML)

**ML** is a subfield of AI that focuses on algorithms and techniques that allow machines to **learn from experience** (typically in the form of data), improving their performance on specific tasks without being explicitly programmed for every scenario.

The widely accepted definition comes from **Arthur Samuel** (1959):

> *"Machine learning is the science of getting computers to act without being explicitly programmed."*

## Deep Learning (DL)

**DL** is a specialized area within ML that centers on the use of **artificial neural networks**, particularly those with many layers (hence "deep"). These deep architectures enable models to learn increasingly abstract and complex representations of data.

From a 2015 *Nature* paper:

> *"Deep learning allows computational models that are composed of multiple processing layers to learn representations of data with multiple levels of abstraction."*

**Key idea**: DL learns features directly from raw data, eliminating the need for manual feature engineering. The network progressively builds representations from low-level patterns (edges, textures) up to high-level concepts (faces, objects).

**Why DL outperforms classical ML**:
- Learns hierarchical feature representations end-to-end
- The same architecture can be reused across different tasks simply by retraining with new data
- Scales well with data and computational power (GPUs)
