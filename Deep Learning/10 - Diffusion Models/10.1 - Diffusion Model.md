**Diffusion models** are a class of generative models that learn to generate data by reversing a gradual noise addition process. The key insight is that if we can learn to undo the corruption, we can generate samples from pure noise.

## Forward Pass (Diffusion Process)

The forward pass is the systematic addition of Gaussian noise to data over multiple timesteps. It gradually transforms data from its original distribution into pure noise.

Starting with a clean sample $x_0$ from the data distribution, the forward process applies a series of noise steps $1, 2, ..., T$ where at each step $t$, we add noise scaled by a variance schedule $\beta_t$.

By applying this recursively and leveraging properties of Gaussian distributions:

$$q(x_t | x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}x_{t-1}, \beta_t\mathbf{I}) \implies q(x_{1:T}|x_0) = \prod_{t=1}^{T}q(x_t|x_{t-1})$$
This can be reparameterized in a closed form to directly compute $x_t$ from $x_{t-1}$
$$x_t = \sqrt{1 - \beta_t} \cdot x_{t-1} + \sqrt{\beta_t} \cdot \epsilon, \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$$

We can get directly $x_t$ from $x_0$:
$$x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} \cdot x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \cdot \epsilon$$

where $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}(1 - \beta_s)$ and $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$. This closed-form expression is crucial: it allows us to jump directly to any timestep without computing all intermediate steps, making training efficient.

### Tractability

The forward process is entirely tractable because:
- It's deterministic given $x_0$ and $\epsilon$
- No parameters need to be learned
- Any intermediate $x_t$ can be computed directly from $x_0$

## Reverse Pass (Reverse Diffusion Process)

The reverse pass learns to undo the forward process by iteratively removing noise. It is the generative mechanism that transforms pure noise back into structured data.

While the forward process is deterministic and fixed, the reverse process must be **learned by a neural network**. The network $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)$ predicts the noise that was added at step $t$, and this prediction is used to step backward:

$$x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \beta_t}} \left( x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}} \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \right) + \sigma_t \mathbf{z}$$

where $\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and $\sigma_t$ is the variance of the reverse step. This reverse formula is also in **closed form**, derived from the Markovian structure of the forward process and the assumption that both forward and reverse processes have Gaussian distributions.

### Predicting $\epsilon_{\theta}$

The reverse process follows a **true posterior distribution** that can be derived analytically from Bayes' rule:

$$q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0) = \mathcal{N}(\tilde{\mu}_t(x_t, x_0), \tilde{\beta}_t\mathbf{I})$$

The learned reverse process $p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)$ aims to match this true posterior (which is intractable to compute directly).

This noise prediction objective is equivalent to minimizing the **Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)** on the log-likelihood of the data.

The ELBO decomposes as:

$$\mathcal{L}_{ELBO} = -\log p(x_T) - \sum_{t=2}^{T} D_{KL}\big[q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0) \| p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)\big] - \mathbb{E}_{q(x_1|x_0)}\big[\log p_\theta(x_0|x_1)\big]$$

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $-\log p(x_T)$ | Prior loss (negligible, $x_T \approx \mathcal{N}(0,I)$) |
| $D_{KL}[...]$ | KL divergence between true and learned reverse processes at each step |
| $\mathbb{E}[\log p_\theta(x_0\|x_1)]$ | Reconstruction loss from $x_1$ to $x_0$ |

Training the network to minimize the noise prediction loss directly minimizes these KL divergence terms, making it equivalent to maximizing the ELBO and thus improving the log-likelihood of the model.

### Simplification to Noise Prediction

The KL divergence between Gaussians has a closed form, but the key insight is that it simplifies dramatically. By Bayes' rule, the KL divergence term can be reparameterized in terms of the noise that was added. Skipping the mathematical steps, we obtain a simplified loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{simple} = \mathbb{E}_{x_0 \sim q(x_0), \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I), t \sim \mathcal{U}(1,T)} \left[ \left\| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta\left(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon, t\right) \right\|^2 \right]$$

This elegantly reparameterizes the mean in terms of the noise $\epsilon$: **predict the noise added to $x_0$**. This transforms a complex Bayesian inference problem into a pure regression problem — easy to optimize and remarkably effective.

### Generation via Sampling

After training, generation is performed by:
1. Starting from random noise $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$
2. Iteratively denoising: $x_{t-1} = \text{Denoise}(x_t, t, \theta)$
3. Repeating $T$ times until reaching $x_0$

The number of reverse steps can be adjusted to trade off quality for speed.
