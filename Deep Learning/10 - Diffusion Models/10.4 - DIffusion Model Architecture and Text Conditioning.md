Modern diffusion models use **U-Net** architectures with **residual connections** and **attention mechanisms** to effectively denoise at multiple scales and incorporate external conditioning information.

![[Pasted image 20260708115729.png]]

## U-Net Architecture

The denoising network typically follows a symmetric encoder-decoder structure:
- **Encoder**: Downsampling convolutional blocks with stride or pooling
- **Bottleneck**: Processing at the lowest resolution
- **Decoder**: Upsampling transposed convolutions with skip connections from the encoder

Skip connections are essential: they allow fine-grained spatial information to bypass the bottleneck, improving reconstruction quality.

## Time Conditioning

The diffusion timestep $t$ must be communicated to the network. Common approaches:
- **Sinusoidal embeddings**: Convert $t$ to a fixed-dimensional vector using Fourier features
- **Learned embeddings**: Trainable time embeddings
- **Conditional batch normalization**: Modulate normalization parameters based on time

These embeddings are typically added to feature maps via affine transformations or residual connections.

## Text Conditioning

For text-to-image generation, external text must be incorporated using a **frozen text encoder** (e.g., CLIP or T5)
