**Stable Diffusion** is a practical, open-source text-to-image diffusion model that achieves high-quality generation with computational efficiency by operating in a **compressed latent space** rather than pixel space.

![[Pasted image 20260708115912.png]]

## Latent Diffusion

Instead of diffusing in the raw pixel space ($256 \times 256 \times 3$), Stable Diffusion first encodes images into a much smaller latent space using a **variational autoencoder (VAE)**:

1. **Encoder**: $x \rightarrow z$ (compress image to latent code, typically $64 \times 64 \times 4$)
2. **Diffusion**: Apply diffusion process to $z$ instead of $x$
3. **Decoder**: $z \rightarrow x$ (reconstruct from latent after denoising)

This reduces computational cost by ~4-9x compared to pixel-space diffusion, enabling fast generation on consumer GPUs.

## Architecture Components

**Text Encoder**: Pre-trained CLIP text encoder converts text prompts into token embeddings that guide generation via cross-attention.

**Denoising U-Net**: The core diffusion model operating in latent space, conditioned on text embeddings through cross-attention layers at multiple resolution levels.

**VAE Decoder**: Converts denoised latent codes back to pixel space.

### Attention Types in the U-Net

Inside the denoising U-Net, two complementary attention mechanisms operate:

1. **Self-Attention** — Over image latents for spatial structure
   - Captures long-range spatial dependencies and coherence within the image
   
2. **Cross-Attention** — From image latents to text tokens for semantics
   - Allows the model to selectively attend to different parts of the text prompt
   - Different image regions can attend to different semantic concepts from the prompt
   - Text embedding plays the role of the conditioning $c$ in Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)

Cross-attention is particularly effective: it enables fine-grained alignment between text concepts and image regions, allowing controlled generation based on semantic content.

## Classifier-Free Guidance

Rather than training a separate classifier, a simpler approach uses a **trained diffusion model** to guide itself by randomly dropping the condition during training (replacing it with a null/empty token).

### Guidance Mechanism

At inference, the conditional and unconditional scores are combined:

$$\nabla_x p(x|c) = \nabla_x p(x) + \gamma \nabla_x p(c|x)$$

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\nabla_x p(x)$ | **Unconditional score** (learned by diffusion model) |
| $\nabla_x p(c\|x)$ | **Classifier score** (how well current sample matches condition) |
| $\gamma$ | **Guidance strength** controlling the influence of conditioning |

The classifier score $\nabla_x p(c|x)$ is obtained implicitly: since the model was trained with random condition dropout, it has learned to estimate whether a sample matches a given condition, without needing a separate classifier.

### Effect of Guidance Strength $\gamma$

- **Higher $\gamma$** → More faithful to the text condition, but lower diversity and potential artifacts
- **Lower $\gamma$** → More diverse generations, but less adherence to the prompt
- **$\gamma = 0$** → Unconditional generation (ignore condition entirely)

This approach is called **Classifier-Free Guidance** because no explicit classifier is needed — the diffusion model itself provides both conditional and unconditional predictions.
