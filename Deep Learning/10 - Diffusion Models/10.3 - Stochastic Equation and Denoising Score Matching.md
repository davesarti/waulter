**Score-based generative models** provide a unified mathematical framework for diffusion models using stochastic differential equations (SDEs) and score functions.

## Score Function

The **score** of a distribution is the gradient of log-probability:

$$s(x) = \nabla_x \log p(x)$$
It points the regions of the space where data is similar to the samples.

### Score-Based Parameterization

An equivalent formulation of the problem predicts the **score** (gradient of log-probability) instead of noise:

$$s_\theta(x_t, t) = -\frac{1}{\sigma_t}\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)$$

where $s_\theta$ is the score network and $\epsilon_\theta$ is the noise prediction network. The score is related to the noise prediction through a simple scaling factor $\sigma_t$.

Since the two parameterizations are related by a scale factor, they are equivalent approaches to the same problem: 
- **Noise prediction** is often easier to work with in practice (simpler scaling, more stable training)
- **Score matching** has stronger theoretical grounding in score-based generative modeling

Both achieve the same goal of learning the reverse process, just using different mathematical representations.

## Score Matching

**Denoising Score Matching** is a training objective that avoids computing log-probabilities:

$$\mathcal{L}_{DSM} = \mathbb{E}_{x, t, \epsilon} \left[ \| \mathbf{s}_\theta(x_t, t) - \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t | x_0) \|_2^2 \right]$$

This is mathematically equivalent to noise prediction in diffusion models but offers better theoretical understanding.

At this point, both forward and reverse process can be formulated as **Stochastic Differential Equations**.
