The **generative trilemma** describes a fundamental tension in generative models: three desirable properties cannot all be maximized simultaneously. Every generative model must make trade-offs among:

1. **High Sample Quality**: Generated samples should be visually realistic and match the data distribution closely.
2. **Diversity**: The model should capture all modes of the data distribution, generating varied outputs rather than mode-collapsing.
3. **Fast Sampling/Inference**: Generation should be computationally efficient, ideally requiring few forward passes.

## Classical Models and the Trilemma

Different generative model families occupy different points in this trade-off space:

- **[[09.1 - Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)|GANs]]**: High quality, high diversity, but poor likelihood estimates
- **[[09.8 - Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)|VAEs]]**: Fast sampling, tractable likelihood, but often lower sample quality
- **Autoregressive models**: Excellent likelihood and quality, but slow sampling
- [[10.1 - Diffusion Model|Diffusion Models]] (with many steps): Excellent quality and diversity, but slow sampling; (with fewer steps): faster but lower quality

## Diffusion Models' Position

Classical diffusion models emphasize quality and diversity over speed, requiring many sequential denoising steps (~1000) to generate high-quality samples. Recent variants explore the boundary by:
- Using distillation to reduce sampling steps
- Employing latent-space diffusion for faster computation
- Developing advanced samplers that maintain quality with fewer steps
